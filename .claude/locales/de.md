# Locale: de (Germany — DACH notes)

Market knowledge for projects with `Language: de` on line 4 of ABOUT.md. Read this
before any client-facing work — copy, UI, forms, checkout, SEO. Anchored on Germany;
Austria and Switzerland share the language but differ on currency (Switzerland uses CHF,
not EUR) and some legal specifics — confirm the target country before assuming either.

## Review mining (customer-voice research)

- German consumers are comparatively review-skeptical and privacy-conscious — expect
  lower review volume than other markets; don't assume abundant review data exists.
- **Trusted Shops** and **ProvenExpert** are German-specific trust-badge/review
  platforms, widely recognized in German e-commerce — a Trusted Shops seal with buyer
  protection ("Käuferschutz") is a real conversion lever if the project has it.

## Legal requirements (Impressum is non-negotiable)

- GDPR ("DSGVO") applies — Germany is the strictest enforcer in the EU. Cookie consent
  must be genuinely opt-in, no pre-checked boxes.
- **Impressum** is a legally required page on virtually every commercial German
  website — lists the responsible party, address and contact details. Its absence is one
  of the most heavily litigated compliance failures in Germany (abmahnfähig — grounds for
  a formal warning/lawsuit from competitors). Treat this as mandatory, not optional, on
  any German site.

## Payment rails

- Germans strongly prefer **"Kauf auf Rechnung"** (buy on invoice, pay after receiving
  goods) and SEPA direct debit ("Lastschrift") — trust in "pay after delivery" runs much
  deeper than in most markets; credit-card usage is comparatively low. PayPal is broadly
  trusted. Klarna is popular for installments. List invoice/PayPal before assuming cards
  are the default.

## WhatsApp and contact channels

- WhatsApp is less dominant as a primary business CTA than in Latin America — Germans
  default to email or a contact form for B2B, phone for urgent matters. WhatsApp Business
  is growing but should not be assumed as the primary channel without confirming with
  the client.

## Formats

- Dates: DD.MM.YYYY (**dots**, not slashes). Currency: 1.234,56 € (dot thousands, comma
  decimal, symbol after — Switzerland uses CHF and a different format, confirm country).
  Phone: +49 XXX XXXXXXX (written 0XXX XXXXXXX domestically).

## Tone notes for de copy

- **"Sie" (formal) is the default and expected register** in professional/B2B copy.
  "Du" (informal) has become common in modern SaaS/tech-brand copy (mirroring how many
  developer tools address users) but is a deliberate choice — confirm it explicitly in
  `## Audience and tone of voice` rather than assuming it is safe.
- Germans research extensively before buying — thorough, transparent legal/terms pages
  (Impressum, AGB, Widerrufsrecht/right of withdrawal) measurably help conversion, not
  just compliance; incomplete legal pages read as untrustworthy, not just risky.

## Local SEO

- Google is dominant, though with a somewhat larger Bing/Windows-default presence than
  in other EU markets — still overwhelmingly Google-first for practical SEO purposes.
- Trust signals (Trusted Shops seal, complete Impressum/AGB, clear Käuferschutz) directly
  affect conversion in a market that researches before it buys.
